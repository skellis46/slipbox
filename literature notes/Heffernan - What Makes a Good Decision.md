# Heffernan - What Makes a Good Decision

_previous note:_ [[Duke - Making Better Choices]]

> The essence of leadership lies in making decisions. Each choice impacts others, no matter how small: whether to work from home today or go into the office; signing off on a piece of work or letting it sit for awhile; even who to copy an email to. Because decisions are primarily about other people, they are freighted with difficulty: it’s impossible always to please everyone. Tradeoffs necessary ensue. Someone’s going to be unhappy.

> She wasn’t on the side of one camp or discipline and came with no pre-determined prescription. What she was great at was posing questions, listening, and watching.

> What lay uppermost in her mind, she told me was this: whatever conclusion her committee, it had to be good enough that people could understand it and believe that it was fair. It’s a good place to begin a definition of a good decision. It doesn’t mean everyone agrees, only that they can live with the outcome. With that as a gold standard, she was swift to dismiss unanswerable debates which would derail the entire discussion.

> Whenever I sit with executives or boards wrestling with tough decisions, I’m struck by the routine, technocratic cost/benefit analysis. Costs are almost always only ever seen as financial and benefits lack impact if they aren’t also framed financially too. And yet any decision has unmeasurable, non-financial consequences. The externalities that turn up in someone else’s balance sheet or in their lives. Market dominance. Reputation. Trust. Standing in the community. The confidence and standing of employees. The ripples flow far and wide. What Warnock, as a philosopher, understood was that it is only in considering all of those that you can coherent choices. Now, in any important decision, my first question is: who get hurt? Someone will. Who? How much? Can it be remediated? If harm is being done, who do we answer the question that always follows any decision: Why? If you can’t clearly explain the why of a decision, it might be endured but it won’t be embraced. Good choices need others to put them into effective action.
