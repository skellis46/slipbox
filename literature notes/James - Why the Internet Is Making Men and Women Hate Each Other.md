# James - Why the Internet Is Making Men and Women Hate Each Other

_previous note:_ [[swissmiss - Disembodied Communication]]

> The Web’s disembodied nature means all of us lose an important connection to reality when we encounter the world through the Internet. None of us are just “users” in the abstract. We have opinions, relationships, duties, experiences, memories, beliefs, loyalties, etc. And all of those things are inflected by our gender. I have no experiences as a daughter or a wife. I have experiences of knowing and seeing daughters and wives, from the perspective of a man. To know the real me, you would have to know me as a man with these realities, not just a user who posts.

> But the Internet repositions us as consumers and posters. Our “self” is abstracted from our embodied life. We post, therefore we exist. We “like,” therefore we believe. This de-gendered, decontextualized way of encountering the world doesn’t make us more likely to be patient with others. It makes us more likely to see things in the starkest, most simplistic terms. Those who post like me are like me. Those who post unlike me and are unlike me. And since the algorithms of social media know that anger is most profitable online emotion, they bombard us not only with the constant sensation of people “like and unlike us,” but with the feeling that those unlike us are our enemies.
