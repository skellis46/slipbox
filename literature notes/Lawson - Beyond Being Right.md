# Lawson - Beyond Being Right

_previous note:_ [[experience matters more than facts]]

> It was good work, and some of it was successful. And yet, we were using the same “rational actor” assumptions that underpin modern economics and capitalism: the idea that people engage with markets in a purely logical way. We were putting facts in front of policymakers and saying, “You need to act on these facts.”

> A couple of researchers have been examining this in organizations. Their research suggests that the more strongly an organization builds its identity around doing good, the more likely it may be to experience various internal problems, from mistreating staff to financial misconduct.

> The biggest thing is to build spaces and mechanisms into the culture of an organization or movement, whatever its scale, that create time and legitimacy for examining the “how,” not only the “what,” and for tending to relationships rather than focusing on tasks all the time.

> This can get messy. People arrive with different experiences of oppression and trauma. There is often a need for more grace, especially when someone who has suffered encounters a person who appears to embody the system that caused the harm.

> Compassion is literally an antidote to fascism.

> We are seeing political forces attack empathy itself and use dehumanization as a tool. We are up against movements funded by billionaires who want a completely deregulated environment and are willing to weaponize a lack of empathy to get there. So compassion matters politically and strategically.
