# James - Briefly in Defense of The Gospel Coalition's AI Policy

_previous note:_ [[Jacobs - Re-Humanization]]

> The bar is worth setting here for several reasons. First, the AI revolution is a question of human identity before it’s a question of human ethics. By that I mean that the most important question anyone needs to ask when they’re thinking about AI is not, “Am I allowed to do this,” but, “What am I, and what is this?” The law of diminishing restriction starts by getting people to forget to ask. There is only one path toward coexisting with AI technology in a non-destructive way, and it goes through this question.

> Already there have been folks online bewailing TGC’s policy for being too strict. I’ll be honest: *I don’t know how to stake out common ground with people who have no interest in asking the existential question with regard to tech.* I’m perfectly comfortable living and working with people who come to different conclusions. But people who believe there is no meaningful reflection to be done are taking themselves and others into a dangerous corner. People who don’t care about a meaningful anthropology, who don’t do any work to answer the question, “Why replace humans this way, but not that way,” are just volunteering their own abolition.

> Here’s my point: If you’re not actively thinking about what this tech *cannot* be or do, you’re not being “realistic.” You’re being transhuman. You’re taking less than five years of innovation as a new baseline of human existence. You’re moving the goalposts of your own personhood. But you don’t have to. You don’t have to have all the answers or the perfect plan. You just have to really do what AI only pretends to do: Think.
