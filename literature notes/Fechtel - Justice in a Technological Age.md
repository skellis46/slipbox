# Fechtel - Justice in a Technological Age

_previous note:_ 

> The whole conflict between the two was made possible by and made lethal by the technology involved: the social media that radicalized and, in some strange way, possibly connected them. This is not to categorically denounce the invention of or prudent use of these technologies, but to once again reveal that the purpose of a technology is what it does, not what its founders and inventors intend. Technology, after all, must exist in the real world and be operated by the humans for whom it is designed.

> When an outside observer approaches the question of whether the participants in a very public incident like this did the right thing, they do so with several advantages. First, they can do so at their leisure. An event that may take two or three seconds is given minutes, hours, or days of careful study. While the participants had only their own perspectives and brief moments in which to react, we are afforded an often panoptic view: multiple angles, high-definition video, and infinite replay-ability. We can also slow these videos down, allowing us to judge real-time events at a speed far slower and more deliberate than the participants were able to enjoy. And critically, we are also able to assess the zeitgeist around an incident before making our own judgment, seeing into the future and, perhaps, if we are honest, making our own assessment calibrated to the prevailing attitude expressed in our own personal information ecosystem.

> Whether it will be possible for terrestrial justice to be done in this situation is uncertain. But if it is possible, it will have to be achieved away from the heat and clamor of social media, away from the imminent threat of death, and separate from the technologies that brought Ms. Good and the ICE officer into conflict and that so fouled up the aftermath. Until then, we may take advantage of a right too-seldom exercised in a world of technologically-enabled maximum choice and optionality: we can choose not to choose; to mourn, lament, and take active care of each other while we await the settling of this conflict.

_reference:_ John Fechtel, Nature/Culture. "Justice in a Technological Age." https://fechtel.substack.com/p/justice-in-a-technological-age
