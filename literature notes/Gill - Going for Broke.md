# Gill - Going for Broke

_previous note:_ [[Gill - Hot Bread Warm Planet]]

> Artisan bakers in America are–as a rule–underpaid, under protected, under appreciated, and overworked. Sadly, we are expected–in an unspoken social contract–to provide the best, most consistent bread for less than the cost of a glass of wine in a restaurant or an oat milk latte at Starbucks.

> But there is something deeper here. This is why I mentioned France, because the cultural and social expectation that bread should be *cheap* is pervasive everywhere. It is this line of thinking that has led to a crisis. And, I think, kept our craft from developing, growing, and maturing like a healthy sourdough starter. How can bakers innovate if we are scared, broke, and tired?

> So just imagine: what if the baker charged the same amount the mixologist charges for a Moscow Mule? There’d be a riot, sure. For the people cruising the Farmers Market with an $8 oat milk latte in hand would gasp at the price of a $12, $15, or $20 sourdough. Never mind that whole grain sourdough bread has 11 essential amino acids, complex carbohydrates, polyunsaturated fats, complex B vitamins, fiber, prebiotics, and will keep for a week.

> I’ve always held that the baker shouldn’t just feed our communities. We should nourish them. But if we’re having problems making payroll, rent, and the utility bill, are we being nourished in return? Because when we don’t charge enough for our bread, we’re not only saying our bread costs less than. As bakers, we’re saying that our worth is less than.

_reference:_ Graison Gill, BreadCrumbs. "Going for Broke." https://graisongill.substack.com/p/going-for-broke
