# Notes to Myself by Seth Godin

Every system exists because someone chose it. Which means someone can choose differently.


Ship the work. Ship it before you feel ready. Ship it while you're still learning. The world needs your imperfect work more than it needs your perfect silence.


Service isn't about self-sacrifice. It's about expanding your awareness beyond your immediate concerns.

10. Silence is an option, and so is leadership The meeting room was full of nodding heads.

Everyone could see the proposal was flawed. No one wanted to be the person who spoke up.

Until someone did.

"I think we need to talk about what we're all thinking," she said.

That moment of courage changed a million-dollar project.

Silence might feel safer. But safe isn't always better.

Leadership isn't a title. It's the decision to say what needs to be said when it needs to be said.


The most effective people don't win arguments. They win understanding.


Treating different people differently requires more work than treating everyone the same. But it's the difference between serving people and processing them.


Uncomfortable facts force us to confront reality instead of managing perceptions. They reveal problems while they're still fixable.

The facts that make us uncomfortable are often the ones we most need to understand.


20. Avoid arguments, embrace conversations Arguments are about winning. Conversations are about learning.

When you argue, your goal is to prove your position is right. This requires you to ignore inconvenient facts and resist changing your mind.

When you converse, your goal is to understand the situation more completely. This lets you ask questions, consider alternatives, and find better solutions.

Arguments entrench positions. Conversations expand possibilities.


Don't let the tyranny of easy measurement distract you from doing important work.


Before you commit to a deadline, consider what you'll have to sacrifice to meet it. Before you agree to a project, understand what other projects you'll have to delay.


Enrollment happens when people understand how your ideas serve their interests, solve their problems, or align with their values.


34. Quitting one thing is the only way to find the focus to do the next thing Every yes makes future nos necessary.

Every commitment reduces capacity for other commitments.

Every project you continue is a project you can't begin.

We add new work without subtracting old work. This creates scattered attention instead of focused impact.

Strategic quitting requires disappointing some people to serve others better.

But quitting inferior opportunities creates space for superior ones.

You can't add your way to focus. You can only subtract your way there.


Sometimes status differences reflect valuable expertise and should be respected. Sometimes they reflect irrelevant factors and should be minimized.


46. Offer dignity Dignity is treating people as worthy of respect regardless of their circumstances or status.

Offering dignity doesn't require agreeing with everyone or giving everyone what they want. It requires treating everyone like their concerns matter and their experience is important.

Dignity is often most important when it's hardest to maintain. When people are frustrated or uninformed. When you have power and they don't.

People remember how you made them feel long after they forget what you said or did.


54. Today's world is unpredictable, and this is as stable as it will ever be again 

We plan as if the future will be similar to the present, only more so.

But change is accelerating. Technology cycles are shortening.

Business models are being disrupted more frequently.

This means adaptability is becoming more valuable than optimization. The ability to respond quickly to changing conditions matters more than the ability to execute perfectly under stable conditions.

Instead of trying to predict what will happen, focus on building capabilities that will be valuable regardless of what happens.

If you're waiting for things to settle down before making important decisions, you'll be waiting indefinitely


57. Invest in skills that compound with effort 

Some skills have expiration dates. They're valuable for specific technologies or trends that will eventually change.

Other skills compound over time. The more you use them, the more valuable they become. They apply across different contexts and remain relevant as circumstances change.

Compound skills include understanding human behavior, clear communication, systematic thinking, and creative problem-solving.

When choosing what to learn, consider not just what's useful now but what will still be useful in ten years.

Compound skills create compound careers.


The alternative is helping people develop tolerance for uncertainty rather than promising to eliminate it.


62.Ideas that spread, win 

The best idea rarely wins. The most spreadable idea wins.

Your brilliant insight that requires twenty minutes to explain will lose to someone's decent insight that takes twenty seconds.

Ideas don't spread because they're true. They spread because they're sticky.

Simple enough to remember. Concrete enough to visualize. Useful enough to share.

If your idea isn't spreading, the problem might not be the idea. The problem might be the packaging.

Make it simpler. Make it stickier. Make it spreadable.

Darwin's insight about evolution was brilliant. "Survival of the fittest" was the spreadable version that stuck.


63. Earn trust through action 

Trust isn't built through promises or explanations.

Trust is built through consistent action over time.

People trust you based on what you do, not what you say you'll do.

Each action either confirms or contradicts the pattern people expect from you. Each choice either strengthens or weakens their confidence in your reliability.

Trust is earned in drops and lost in buckets.

You can't talk your way into trust. You can only act your way into trust.


65. Attitudes are skills 

We treat attitudes like weather-things that happen to us.

But optimism is a learnable skill. So is resilience. So is confidence.

Optimism is the skill of finding opportunity inside problems.

Confidence is the skill of focusing on what you can control instead of what you can't. Resilience is the skill of adapting to setbacks instead of being defeated by them.

Like other skills, attitudes improve with practice.

Some people practice pessimism until they're experts at spotting problems. Others practice gratitude until they're experts at spotting possibilities.

Your attitude is one of the few things you control completely. It's also one of the most important factors determining what happens next.

If attitudes are skills, they can be developed.

What are you practicing?
