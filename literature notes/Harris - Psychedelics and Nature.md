# Harris - Psychedelics and Nature

_previous note:_ [[Kimmerer - Serviceberry Abundance and Reciprocity]]

> ‘Nature’ is perhaps the most complex word in our language, and its meaning is tied to our worldview. The environmental philosopher Neil Evernden suggested that it emphasizes a “sense of separation between the human subject and the surrounding field of natural objects” (Evernden, 1992). Anthropologist Tim Ingold concurs, suggesting that “nature” only exists “for a being that does not belong there, and that can look upon it, in the manner of the detached scientist” (Ingold, 2000). Many Indigenous languages have no single abstract term for ‘nature’ as a realm separate from people and culture, and the concepts they do have are relational.

_reference:_ Adrian Harris, Body Mind Place. "Psychedelics and Nature." https://adrianharris.org/blog/2026/10/27/psychedelics-and-nature/
