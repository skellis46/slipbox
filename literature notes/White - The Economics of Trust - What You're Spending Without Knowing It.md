# White - The Economics of Trust - What You're Spending Without Knowing It

_previous note:_ [[White - Becoming a Master of Trustworthiness]]

> **What Happens When You Trust Nurses, Not Bureaucracy**
>
> In the Netherlands, a healthcare organization called *Buurtzorg* removed middle management entirely.
>
> No bosses. No command chains. Just small teams of nurses who decide how to care for patients, together.
>
> The result?
>
> •   Faster recoveries
> •   Lower costs
> •   Happier teams
>
> They rewrote the rules by doing something radical: **trusting professionals to be professional**.
>
> It’s a reminder that systems don’t create accountability—*relationships* do.

> **Trust is a currency. And most leaders are spending it like it’s unlimited.**
>
> Here’s what that looks like in practice:
>
> •   Outsourcing customer care to people with no power to fix anything.
> •   Marketing promises that the product can’t live up to.
> •   Saying “we value people” while laying off hundreds by email.
>
> It’s not evil. It’s **unconscious**.
>
> But it’s not sustainable.

> **Trust Isn’t Free—But Losing It Is Expensive**
>
> We say trust matters, but here’s the truth: **most companies don’t measure it**, and fewer know how to build it. They track churn, engagement, revenue—but not what it feels like to be in a relationship with them.

> In a world full of PR gloss, **honesty feels revolutionary**.
>
> **You’re Always Sending a Signal**
>
> Every action—every policy, every trade-off—is a signal.
>
> You’re either building trust or spending it. There’s no neutral.
>
> And when trust runs out?
>
> •   Customers don’t complain. They *leave*.
> •   Employees don’t push back. They *check out*.
> •   Stakeholders don’t argue. They *withdraw*.
>
> You don’t get an alert. You just wake up one day with a credibility problem that’s already cost you more than you know.

> **What Now?**
>
> Start here:
>
> •   What decisions am I making that erode trust—without me realizing it?
> •   Where have I chosen efficiency over empathy?
> •   What would change if I treated trust like my most important asset?
>
> Then ask the question most leaders never do:
>
> **Would I trust me?**
