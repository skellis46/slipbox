# Popova - The Measure of a Rich Life - Wendell Berry on the Key to Mirth Under Hardship and Delight as a Force of Resistance to Consumerism

_previous note:_ [[Popova - Wendell Berry on Solitude and the Two Great Enemies of Creative Work]]

> A generation later, the poet, farmer, and ecological steward **Wendell Berry** — a poet in the largest Baldwinian sense — picked up the time-escalated quarrel in his slim, large-spirited book ***The Hidden Wound*** to offer, without looking away from its scarring realities, a healing and conciliatory direction of resistance to a culture in which our enjoyment of life is taken from us by the not-enoughness at the hollow heart of consumerism, only to be sold back to us at the price of the latest product, and sold in discriminating proportion along lines of stark income inequality.

_reference:_ Maria Popova, The Marginalian. "The Measure of a Rich Life: Wendell Berry on the Key to Mirth Under Hardship and Delight as a Force of Resistance to Consumerism." https://www.themarginalian.org/2019/11/17/wendell-berry-hidden-wound/
