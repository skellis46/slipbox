# festschrift

There's a thing in academia called a _Festschrift_ which means (in German) "celebratory writing". Usually it's a collection of essays, articles etc that are published to honour the work of an academic towards the end of their career. 

I'd never heard of the word until about two years ago when a colleague here at C-DaRE (Centre for Dance Research) suggested we organise one for the remarkable human being (and dance scholar) [Sarah Whatley](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/persons/sarah-whatley/).

On Thursday 15 October C-DaRE hosted an all-day event which was loosely a _Festschrift_. I say _loosely_ because although it was celebratory it was by no means a collection of essays etc. Instead, it was a wonderfully chaotic day of performances, conversations, videos, eating, dancing and (inevitably?) learning to tap dance. It was beautiful, funny and deeply moving. 

Sarah didn't know much about the day beforehand other than 'something was happening' and she was genuinely and hilariously surprised when she walked into the venue. People had travelled from all over and, well, there were a lot of tears. 

[image by Kathryn Stamp]

I'm writing about this here (and now) because as much as the event was a way to celebrate Sarah's extraordinary and still ongoing career, the day also spoke volumes about the team Sarah built and nourished and its willingness to spend time together being serious and being silly.  

It struck me afterwards that a _Festschrift_ is usually meant to be writing, and yet a room full of dance people did what people everywhere do when something matters: they danced. The DJ and producer Theo Parrish once said that escapism is how outsiders describe dancing, but “solidarity is what it really offers.” Solidarity is a crowd of people learning to tap dance on a Thursday afternoon.

The philosopher Michael Sandel has written about the strange belief, at the heart of meritocracy, that those who succeed deserve their success – as if talent, timing and the people around us had nothing to do with it. A _Festschrift_ is, in its usual sense, a meritocratic form: it gathers up a career and hands it back to one person. What happened that day quietly and boisterously exposed how odd that idea is. Sarah’s work has never been hers alone and she never fails to say so quickly and generously.
