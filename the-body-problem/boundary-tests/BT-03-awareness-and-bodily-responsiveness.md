# BT-03: Awareness as a boundary marker

Status: **question agreed – boundary test not yet conducted**  
Opened: 8 October 2026

## Agreed question

> Can the map distinguish conceptions of embodiment for which awareness is necessary from those for which it is not?

Question agreed on 8 October 2026. **No outcome or change to the map has been agreed.**

## Agreed testing structure – 14 October 2026

BT-03 remains **one boundary test** with two separately recorded but interdependent analytical strands. Neither strand is an agreed finding.

- **BT-03a – discriminatory capacity:** Test whether awareness, as understood by the sources, differentiates conceptions of embodiment. Distinguish necessary, optional and unnecessary awareness, and explicit authorial claims from our inferences.
- **BT-03b – conceptual premises:** Using the same accounts, examine what each takes embodiment to mean, what it seeks to explain and how those premises condition awareness's role.

**Agreed sequence:** (1) examine BT-03a provisionally, (2) examine BT-03b using the same cases, (3) revisit the conclusions of BT-03a in the light of BT-03b, and only then assess what the current map can or cannot represent. A positive result for either strand does not entail a positive result for the other.

**Case-selection rigour is central.** Do not select cases merely because their contrasting positions on awareness are expected in advance. Establish source-specific definitions, explanatory scope and evidence before classifying. Include at least one genuinely difficult or equivocal case that could undermine the proposed distinction. Keep theory, empirical demonstration and pedagogical practice analytically distinct. Selection criteria and exclusions must be documented before inferring findings. No case set has yet been agreed, and no map revision is authorised.

## Scope clarification – 14 October 2026

**Agreed:** Lux et al. (2021) motivated the maps through curiosity rather than supplying a privileged foundation. Progress and proportionate rigour matter more than preserving those original categories. Case selection matters, but a case's position inside, at the edge of or outside the map is an outcome of analysis, not a predetermined label.

**Still provisional:** Concentrate initially on the map and its edges while allowing genuinely outside cases. Whether to pursue external cases systematically later is unresolved. The four-case design below remains optional rather than binding. Record selection reasons in advance without prejudging awareness or map fit.

## Case-selection protocol – proposal, 14 October 2026 (not agreed)

**Purpose:** Assess the two BT-03 strands without selecting accounts for their expected answers. Case selection itself requires scrutiny before any texts are designated.

1. **Unit:** A specific account of embodiment grounded in an identifiable primary text or tightly related texts, not an entire tradition. Record each source's explanatory object, account of embodiment, treatment of awareness, type of claim and evidence. Silence on awareness is not evidence of rejection.
2. **Initial pool:** Identify accounts through differing *explanatory orientations* among the map's existing conceptions, without first coding them as awareness-required or awareness-unnecessary. Log what was considered and why any account was excluded.
3. **Comparison structure:** Combine cross-conception variation with at least one close comparison between accounts sharing theoretical commitments, so that a contrast cannot be attributed only to entirely different explanatory purposes.
4. **Critical case:** Include an equivocal or potentially disconfirming account capable of challenging awareness's discriminatory power, and retain it if inconvenient.
5. **Initial scale:** Tentatively four accounts with roles: bodily organisation, lived bodily experience, a closely related comparator and a difficult case. These are selection functions, **not** new map categories; no particular authors or texts are yet selected.
6. **Interpretation:** For BT-03a distinguish awareness necessary to the account, relevant to some investigated phenomena, optional or not required by the explanation. Do not equate absence of evidence, absence of report, absence of reflective attention and absence of consciousness. In BT-03b interrogate each account's premises and re-check BT-03a's classifications.
7. **Possible outcomes:** A meaningful distinction, no distinction, source-level indeterminacy, conceptual non-comparability or a distinction that does not add explanatory value beyond existing categories are all legitimate.
8. **Map assessment last:** Record what is representable, merely visually absent, strained or incompatible without modifying the map in advance.

This protocol is an **assistant proposal**, not an agreed methodology or finding. Case identities, corpus boundaries and exclusion decisions require discussion.

## Working distinctions, not settled definitions

- **Awareness may be equivalent to consciousness.** This is an open working possibility, not a definition imposed on all accounts.
- Perception and awareness are distinct terms. Research on blindsight and masked perception suggests that perceptual processing can occur without reported awareness, but whether *perception itself* can be unconscious remains contested. Do not treat the stronger claim as settled.
- Self-awareness or the sense of an 'I' is not synonymous with consciousness. The kind of experience described as *being danced* in Ellis's *Threshold of the Self* is pertinent but does not resolve this issue.
- A question about the *content* of consciousness (body or environment) is not this boundary test. No assumption that awareness is necessary for every conception of embodiment.

## Earlier proposed test question (not agreed)

> Can the map distinguish bodily responsiveness that does not require conscious attention from forms of bodily awareness deliberately cultivated in somatic practice, without making awareness a general condition of embodiment?

This is a **proposed working question**, not an agreed formulation. In BT-02, the originating question was whether a passive walker could be said to be *aware of* its environment rather than merely having its behaviour conditioned by it. That question remains an impetus, not a claim that the walker has awareness.

## Why this is a separate test

BT-02 examined how a nonliving body's organisation and environmental coupling contribute to locomotion. BT-03 examines what different accounts mean by **awareness**. It should not retrospectively impose awareness as a requirement on BT-02.

One co-author approaches embodiment primarily through somatic practice and felt sense. This is a useful perspective but may encourage conflation of embodiment, awareness and consciously attended sensation. The test must permit accounts that separate these.

## Bounded cases for comparison

### A. Mechanically conditioned locomotion

McGeer's passive walker responds dynamically to the slope, gravity and contact with the ground. Those relationships are not, by themselves, evidence of phenomenal experience, conscious attention, explicit environmental representations or awareness. In the engineering paper, sensitivity to parameter changes is a property of the dynamic behaviour, not a report about what the walker experiences.

### B. Directed bodily attention in Feldenkrais Awareness Through Movement

The Feldenkrais Guild UK describes *Awareness Through Movement* as somatic practice using guided movement and attentive exploration of bodily sensations. This is an appropriate specific example in which **attending to movement and sensation** is an aim of the pedagogy. A professional body's account of its aims should not be treated as empirical evidence of precisely what every participant experiences.

Source: Feldenkrais Guild UK, 'What is the Feldenkrais Method?' https://www.feldenkrais.co.uk/about-feldenkrais/about-the-feldenkrais-method/

### C. Performing skilfully without attending to the body

Shaun Gallagher (2011) discusses a possible 'performative forgetting' of the body during skilled activity and asks how somatic reflection differs from pre-reflective bodily awareness. Awareness directed towards the body can affect performance in more than one way; attending is not simply equivalent to greater skill or greater embodiment.

Source: Gallagher, S. (2011). 'Somaesthetics and the Care of the Body.' *Metaphilosophy*, 42(3), 305–313. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9973.2011.01686.x. The claims recorded here are grounded in the published abstract; full-text interpretation requires checking the paper.

For body schema and body image, a further conceptual source is Gallagher, S. (2005), *How the Body Shapes the Mind*, Oxford University Press. https://doi.org/10.1093/0199271941.001.0001. Its introduction distinguishes proprioceptive information from proprioceptive awareness. Do not project human body-schema terminology onto passive mechanical structures without an argument.

## Distinctions to examine, not yet categories

- **Physical coupling and responsiveness:** movement depends on conditions of contact or environmental change, without a demonstrated capacity to experience those conditions.
- **Sensorimotor organisation without conscious monitoring:** an organism's movement can be organised without ongoing reflective attention. Do not equate this with the walker's entirely mechanical organisation.
- **Pre-reflective bodily awareness:** a contested phenomenological concept, not interchangeable with either neural processing or deliberate introspection.
- **Attending to bodily sensation:** an explicit pedagogical concern in somatic practice, not a necessary condition of all movement or every conception of embodiment.

These are questions about how terms operate in different accounts. They are **not** proposed new map axes or a linear hierarchy leading to consciousness.

## First-pass locations on the existing map – provisional

- The Feldenkrais case intersects **interoception / feeling / selfhood**, **lived body / phenomenology** and **bodily constraint / affordance**, with first-person and practice-based / pedagogical knowledge modes.
- Gallagher's conceptual questions cross **lived body / phenomenology** and **cognition / language / simulation** and may require theoretical / conceptual knowing, as well as evidence from movement or performance.
- The passive walker remains under **bodily constraint / affordance**; the Lux levels may fit poorly, as already recorded in BT-02.

## Potential failure point to test

The map offers positions for all three cases but may not clearly mark **which sense of awareness is being asserted, by whom or on what evidence**. This could be a limitation of its visual resolution, a cross-cutting distinction between conceptions or an issue in the underlying categories. It is **not yet** a demonstrated failure.

Another risk is mistaking the mode by which knowledge is produced (e.g. first-person reflection) for a property required of the body under discussion (e.g. an experiencing subject).

## Reconsideration after reading *Threshold of the Self* (provisional, 8 October 2026)

Source read: Simon Ellis, [*Threshold of the Self: Dancing and Non-dual Awareness*](../../improvisation%20and%20the%20threshold%20of%20the%20self.md), working chapter in the repository (`improvisation and the threshold of the self.md`). In particular, 'Authentic movement: awareness while dancing', 'Deep sensory dancing', 'Being danced', 'Making sense of the phenomena' and 'The practice: attention and precision weighting'. This source was supplied as a way of understanding the skills at stake in somatic practice, not as evidence for universal claims about all somatics.

**User's stated inclination, not a settled definition:** awareness may best be treated as consciousness. Awareness of the body and of the environment would then indicate different contents of consciousness rather than fundamentally different concepts of awareness. Perception should remain distinct: it need not always be conscious. The earlier proposed contrast of body-awareness versus environment-awareness may therefore be misplaced.

**What the writing shows about skill:** the authentic movement practiced by Ellis and Katye Coe was *autotelic*, with no prescribed movement sequence, aim to produce a piece of movement or external standard of success. 'What kind of expertise is involved in a practice whose activity consists not simply in generating movement but in attending and allowing movement to emerge without judgement?' is the explicit question in the chapter. The provisional hypothesis is that somatic expertise develops alongside attentional expertise: sustained sensing, openness to sensory variation and reduced goal-directed expectation. These are situated interpretations, not experimentally validated outcomes, and the chapter states that its predictive-processing account does not explain the apparent transition into *being danced*.

The chapter distinguishes *deep sensory dancing*, where heightened sensory awareness retains a self as organiser, from *being danced*, where awareness is described as continuing without feeling owned in the usual way. The latter is identified retrospectively upon a return to a sense of self. Therefore, neither reduced self-awareness nor the loss of explicit agency should be conflated with absence of consciousness; retrospective interpretation is itself a limit.

**Gallagher caution:** Gallagher (2011) questions how somatic reflection relates to expert performance and how bodily attention may improve or disrupt it. His paper should not be represented as establishing that all skilled activity has a measurable end-product. The useful methodological contrast is between expertise recognised through identifiable task outcomes and practices whose cultivated abilities are more difficult to specify or judge externally. Do not assert that somatic capacities are inherently unmeasurable.

Related note in the repository: [*Confusing self-consciousness with consciousness*](../../confusing%20self-consciousness%20with%20consciousness.md) draws on Anil Seth to distinguish conscious level, contents and self and explicitly notes that perception may be conscious or unconscious.
