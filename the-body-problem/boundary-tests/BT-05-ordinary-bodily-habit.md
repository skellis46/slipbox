**Summary** *(LLM (Claude), 26 November 2026)*: you walk into a familiar room in the dark and reach straight for the light switch without thinking. Can the map describe an ordinary habit like this?

**Origin** *(SE, 26 November 2026)*: SE proposed a simpler test as a break from wading into ontological and epistemological debates with the LLM every time; those debates did not seem to push the enquiry forward and, for the most part, were being rehashed. It was not a rule that all tests should be simpler.

**Where it stands** *(LLM (Claude), 19 and 26 November 2026)*: yes, the map describes it easily, using the philosopher Maurice Merleau-Ponty's account of the body as lived and familiar with its world. Because the fit was so easy, the test shows the map is useful for describing things but did not challenge it much. Also noted: practice and repetition build bodily skills, but that does not mean someone becomes more embodied (see [BT-04](BT-04-degrees-of-embodiment.md)).

## Agreed question

> Can the existing map adequately describe an ordinary bodily habit without requiring additional conceptual categories?

## Bounded example

Entering a familiar room in the dark and reaching for a light switch without explicitly calculating its position. This illustration was generated by LLM for the discussion; it is not Merleau-Ponty's own example.

## Primary source

Merleau-Ponty, M. (1945/2012). *Phenomenology of Perception*. Translated by D. A. Landes. Routledge. See 'The Spatiality of One's Own Body and Motricity', especially the discussion of habit and examples of typing, organ playing and using a walking stick. His account treats bodily habit as practical familiarity with the world that is not reducible to either explicit intellectual knowledge or a mechanical series of reflexes. This is a source-based summary; the switch example is LLM's, not Merleau-Ponty's.

## Map application

- **Conceptions:** strong fit with conception 6, lived body / phenomenology (the body as perspective and lived experience). Conception 2, bodily constraint / affordance (bodily capacities shape action and cognition), is a plausible secondary location; this is an interpretation of the map, not a claim by Merleau-Ponty.
- **Levels of investigation:** sensorimotor organisation and action are relevant (Lux's level 4, sensory and motor activity: *reading added by LLM (Claude), 26 November 2026*). A phenomenological account is not simply reducible to Lux et al.'s biological levels.
- **Evidence / knowledge modes:** "theoretical or conceptual work" is central to this source. "Self-report or first-person data" and "practice-based or pedagogical knowledge" are possible additional modes, not evidence supplied by the example.

## Agreed provisional finding (SE and LLM)

Ordinary bodily habit can be accommodated within the existing map, principally through lived body / phenomenology. Merleau-Ponty's account shows how practical bodily familiarity develops through repetition, learning and practice without requiring explicit knowledge of the spatial organisation of an action. The test demonstrates the map's usefulness for describing things more than its power to tell things apart, and does not warrant a map change.

This again raises the role of training and practice in *developing* embodiment. However, developing an embodied capacity is not necessarily becoming *more embodied*, as distinguished in [BT-04](BT-04-degrees-of-embodiment.md).

## Deferred questions

- Distinguishing bodily knowing from being able to articulate or describe that knowing.
- Distinguishing **kinds of knowing** from the map's **evidence and knowledge modes**, which name how a phenomenon is investigated rather than necessarily what kind of knowledge is involved.

Both bear directly on the body problem. They are kept in the [candidate pool](../future-tests/candidate-pool.md) (as C06 and C07) rather than opened as active tests; C07 was later split, and part of it became [BT-07](BT-07-knowing-and-evidence.md). The wider proposal that an ideal boundary test should both examine the map and develop the chapter's argument is recorded there as a selection preference.

## Limitation

Because lived body / phenomenology already appears on the map, this was a comparatively easy fit. Its limited power to tell things apart is a finding about the selected test, not proof that habit is philosophically straightforward. No claim is made that the two deferred questions have been resolved.
