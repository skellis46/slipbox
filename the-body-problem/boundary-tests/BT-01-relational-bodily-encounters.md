# BT-01: Relational bodily encounters

Status: open. Test question agreed, finding not settled.

## Agreed question

> Can the existing map accommodate relational bodily encounters in which sensing, sense-making and meaning may be differently constituted for each participant, including situations where these processes are asymmetrical or not consciously experienced?

## Cases under consideration

- Elisabeth Hsu's historical study of tactile examination in early Chinese medicine, particularly practices concerning *mai*.
- Feldenkrais Functional Integration as a contemporary practice involving tactile or kinaesthetic interaction.
- Massage was mentioned as another potential modern case but has not been investigated.

These are different cases and cannot be treated as interchangeable evidence. Hsu's historical analysis should not be conflated with first-hand descriptions of either practitioner's or patient's sensations.

## Initial map application, not a finding

The map offers possible categories for relationality / sense-making and lived body / phenomenology. Lux's levels 4, 5 and 7 appear relevant to sensory-motor activity, perception and social interaction. Practice-based or pedagogical knowledge may apply to a contemporary somatic case. Historical analysis is not clearly represented by the existing knowledge-mode labels.

The diagram does not explicitly differentiate whose sensing, knowing or meaning is at issue. This might be a limitation of visual resolution, rather than a conceptual boundary. Test that rather than presuming failure.

## Cautions

Sensing is not identical to sense-making or meaning. Bodily contact does not establish reciprocal meaning. The absence of testimony about a patient's experience is not evidence that the patient had none. Intersubjectivity is already addressed in embodiment scholarship, including Lux et al.
