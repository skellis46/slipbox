**Summary** *(LLM (Claude), 26 November 2026)*: when one person touches another, as in pulse-taking or a hands-on somatic lesson, can the map show that each person may sense and understand the encounter differently?

**Origin** *(SE, 26 November 2026)*: the test began from a hunch that a practice like early Chinese medicine might not fit easily within Lux's thinking. The question then shifted to who is touching and who is being touched: is the embodied understanding of the expert (the practitioner) distinct from the potential effect on the patient?

**Where it stands** *(LLM (Claude), 19 and 26 November 2026)*: an early observation is that the map does not show *whose* sensing or understanding is in question. SE and SdH have not decided whether that is a real problem or just a limit of how the map is drawn.

## Agreed question

> Can the existing map accommodate relational bodily encounters in which sensing, sense-making and meaning may be differently constituted for each participant, including situations where these processes are asymmetrical or not consciously experienced?

## Cases under consideration

- Elisabeth Hsu's historical study of tactile examination in early Chinese medicine, particularly practices concerning *mai* (the vessels or pulses felt by the practitioner).
- Feldenkrais Functional Integration (one-to-one, hands-on lessons in the Feldenkrais Method) as a contemporary practice involving tactile or kinaesthetic interaction.
- Massage was mentioned as another potential modern case but has not been investigated.

These are different cases and cannot be treated as interchangeable evidence. Hsu's historical analysis should not be conflated with first-hand descriptions of either practitioner's or patient's sensations.

## Initial map application, not a finding

The map offers possible categories for conception 5, relationality / sense-making (embodied interaction constitutes meaning), and conception 6, lived body / phenomenology (the body as perspective and lived experience). Three of Lux's seven levels appear relevant: level 4, sensory and motor activity; level 5, integrative sensations, (intero)perception and discrimination; and level 7, social and cultural interaction. The evidence / knowledge mode "practice-based or pedagogical knowledge" may apply to a contemporary somatic case. Historical analysis is not clearly represented by any of the six evidence / knowledge modes.

The map does not show whose sensing, knowing or meaning is at issue. This might be a representational limitation (the map's categories can hold the difference but the drawing cannot show it) rather than a conceptual boundary (showing it would distort the account or change the map); see the distinctions in the [current summary](../current-summary.md#distinctions-to-retain). Which of these it is has not yet been tested, so it should not be assumed to be a failure of the map.

## Cautions

*Written by LLM during the exchange with SE.*

Sensing is not identical to sense-making or meaning. Bodily contact does not establish reciprocal meaning. The absence of testimony about a patient's experience is not evidence that the patient had none. Intersubjectivity is already addressed in embodiment scholarship, including Lux et al.
