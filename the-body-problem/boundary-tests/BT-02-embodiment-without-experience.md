# BT-02: Embodiment without experience

Status: **open – direction agreed, finding not settled**  
Opened: 8 October 2026

## Working question

> What conception of embodiment makes it possible to describe the passive walker as embodied, and what might that conception exclude?

This is the question proposed at the end of the discussion of BT-02. The proposal to retain the passive walker and examine this question was agreed. The wording itself has not been separately finalised.

## Case

Tad McGeer's passive dynamic walker shows how a suitably configured mechanical structure can produce a walking gait downhill through its interactions with gravity and the ground, without active central control. It is not navigating by selecting destinations or routes. Environmental interaction here means physical coupling with slope and terrain, not intentional navigation.

Lux et al. (2021) explicitly discuss passive dynamic walking in their account of embodied robotics. This means that the case is not an accidental addition from outside the literature informing our maps.

## Why this case matters

The discussion reached a shared working position: **some conceptions of embodiment do not require a living or experiencing body**. This is not a claim that all theories of embodiment accept nonliving systems. It means that life or first-person experience cannot be assumed to be necessary conditions for every conception represented on the map.

The implications for dance and somatics are potentially significant but remain unexplored. Neither experience nor embodied knowing has been declared irrelevant to those fields.

## Attempt at locating the case

- **Conception:** bodily constraint / affordance appears promising, because mechanical organisation and environmental interaction contribute to action.
- **Lux level:** sensory and motor activity (4) is the nearest category, but the passive walker illustrates locomotion without sensory processing or control in the usual robotic sense.
- **Knowledge mode:** experimental measurement and theoretical or computational modelling may be relevant; the map's existing labels should be checked against the actual study.
- **Disciplinary colour:** robotics is not separately designated in the working legend.

These are preliminary placements. They do not establish either a successful fit or a boundary.

## Discarded or qualified arguments

- A simple pendulum was suggested as a counterexample and rejected as too weak a comparison. It does not involve the same form of locomotion through contact with terrain. Avoid using it as a decisive comparator.
- The suggestion that inclusion should depend on what an account 'contributes to understanding' was withdrawn as an inadequate boundary criterion.
- Do not equate environmental interaction with conscious perception or intentional navigation.
- Do not infer a single universal definition of embodiment from Lux et al.'s treatment of embodied robotics.

## Tension to preserve

The map should be permissive enough to trouble normative assumptions about embodiment. Yet if everything counts as embodied, the map loses discriminating power. This tension is not yet resolved, and is part of what BT-02 is meant to investigate.
