# BT-04: Degrees of embodiment

Status: **question proposed, distinction agreed – test not yet conducted**  
Opened: 14 October 2026

## Provocation (not evidence or a theoretical position adopted for this test)

> “Writing is the most disembodied art, and reading and writing are largely private and solitary experiences, so music and dance have always enchanted me as arts in which the body of the performer communicates directly to the audience, welding a kind of communion writers rarely experience.”

Rebecca Solnit, *A Field Guide to Getting Lost*.

**Role of quotation:** This passage was simply the provocation from which BT-04 emerged. The enquiry does **not** aim to evaluate Solnit's relative judgements about writing, reading, music or dance, nor does it take her statement as an established theory or evidence for degrees of embodiment.

## Proposed test question (not yet agreed)

> Can something be more or less embodied?

## Agreed analytical distinction

- **Degrees of embodiment:** Whether it is meaningful to say that something is *more or less embodied*.
- **Degrees of bodily involvement:** Whether bodily activity, contribution, visibility or participation can vary without warranting a judgement that embodiment itself varies.

This separation is useful to the enquiry but does not yet settle whether either comparison is conceptually valid, how degrees would be assessed or which accounts could sustain it.

## Scope

Test whether different conceptions of embodiment make degrees intelligible, rather than treating differences between art forms as the object of investigation. Avoid collapsing the question back into the map's general concern with what counts as embodiment. Specific accounts, selection method and outcomes have **not** been agreed.

## Further discussion – 14 October 2026 (working distinctions, no finding)

- **Agreed correction:** Neither the quantity of movement, the number of bodily parts involved nor bodily morphology should be used as a proxy or direct measure for embodiment. The body's contribution to an activity is not equivalent to embodiment itself.
- **Promising possibility, not settled:** Variation may occur along particular dimensions without licensing an overall ranking from less to more embodied.
- **Proposed sharper test:** Investigate whether *the same activity* can become more or less embodied over time. If a genuine change in embodiment can be shown, that would support the possibility of degrees in at least that conception. But distinguish *a change in embodiment* from *a change in capacities occurring through embodiment*.
- Comparison across different activities remains interesting but is not the first priority. No specific case, criterion or finding has been agreed.
