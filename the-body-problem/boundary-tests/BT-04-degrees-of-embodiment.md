# BT-04: Degrees of embodiment

Status: **active – two agreed provisional findings, third contrasting conception next**  
Opened: 14 October 2026

## Provocation (not evidence or a theoretical position adopted for this test)

> “Writing is the most disembodied art, and reading and writing are largely private and solitary experiences, so music and dance have always enchanted me as arts in which the body of the performer communicates directly to the audience, welding a kind of communion writers rarely experience.”

Rebecca Solnit, *A Field Guide to Getting Lost*.

**Role of quotation:** This passage was simply the provocation from which BT-04 emerged. The enquiry does **not** aim to evaluate Solnit's relative judgements about writing, reading, music or dance, nor does it take her statement as an established theory or evidence for degrees of embodiment.

## Proposed test question (not yet agreed)

> Can something be more or less embodied?

## Agreed analytical distinction

- **Degrees of embodiment:** Whether it is meaningful to say that something is *more or less embodied*.
- **Degrees of bodily involvement:** Whether bodily activity, contribution, visibility or participation can vary without warranting a judgement that embodiment itself varies.

This separation is useful to the enquiry but does not yet settle whether either comparison is conceptually valid, how degrees would be assessed or which accounts could sustain it.

## Scope

Test whether different conceptions of embodiment make degrees intelligible, rather than treating differences between art forms as the object of investigation. Avoid collapsing the question back into the map's general concern with what counts as embodiment. Specific accounts, selection method and outcomes have **not** been agreed.

## Further discussion – 14 October 2026 (working distinctions, no finding)

- **Agreed correction:** Neither the quantity of movement, the number of bodily parts involved nor bodily morphology should be used as a proxy or direct measure for embodiment. The body's contribution to an activity is not equivalent to embodiment itself.
- **Promising possibility, not settled:** Variation may occur along particular dimensions without licensing an overall ranking from less to more embodied.
- **Proposed sharper test:** Investigate whether *the same activity* can become more or less embodied over time. If a genuine change in embodiment can be shown, that would support the possibility of degrees in at least that conception. But distinguish *a change in embodiment* from *a change in capacities occurring through embodiment*.
- Comparison across different activities remains interesting but is not the first priority. No specific case, criterion or finding has been agreed.

## First conceptual comparison – 14 October 2026 (assistant interpretation, not agreed)

Working question: **When an activity changes over time, can any conception in our map explain that change as a change in the degree of embodiment, rather than simply a change in bodily capacities or experience?** This is a working reformulation, not a newly agreed replacement for the original proposed question.

Three provisional comparisons, using the map's conception labels:

1. **Lived body / phenomenology:** The same improvisational activity can change as habits and lived relations to movement change. This does not by itself entail that the activity becomes *more embodied*. Example is illustrative, not an empirical result.
2. **Relationality / sense-making:** The embodied action account of Varela, Thompson and Rosch permits changing sensorimotor-environment relationships and emergent cognitive structures. Whether there is a degree of embodiment rather than a reorganisation of embodied activity is unsettled. Source: *The Embodied Mind*, 'Enaction: Embodied Cognition', https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/9780262529365.003.0008 (publisher abstract consulted).
3. **Biological incorporation:** Krieger's account of embodiment as socially and ecologically shaped biological incorporation is intrinsically historical. Accumulating or changing incorporated effects does not entail a person is more embodied. Source: Krieger (2005), https://doi.org/10.1136/jech.2004.024562 (open-access primary article consulted).

**Provisional issue:** The examples demonstrate meaningful change within embodied processes but do not yet establish a scalar measure of embodiment. Neither a single ordering nor the impossibility of degrees is proved. A multidimensional account might permit degrees within dimensions without a defensible overall ranking, but only if the dimensions are genuinely dimensions *of embodiment*, not merely bodily phenomena grouped under that heading.

**Methodological restraint:** Do not treat the three map conception labels as internally uniform theories, or the movement-practice illustration as evidence. The source texts do not directly adjudicate the degrees question. No substantive finding and no map revision.

## Agreed provisional findings – 16 October 2026

### 1. Biological incorporation

**Conception:** Biological incorporation. Embodiment understood through incorporation of material, social and environmental conditions into biological organisation and functioning across time (a provisional map characterisation, not Krieger's exact definition).

**Account considered:** Nancy Krieger (2005), 'Embodiment: a conceptual glossary for epidemiology', *Journal of Epidemiology & Community Health*, 59(5), 350–355, https://doi.org/10.1136/jech.2004.024562. Selected for its account of historical biological incorporation. It is illustrative, not exhaustive or representative by fiat.

**Agreed provisional finding (SE–LLM, not Krieger's claim):**

> Within the conception of biological incorporation, differences in bodily organisation, capacities and embodied experience can be described across time. It is conceivable that these differences bear some relationship to degrees of embodiment. However, the nature of that relationship remains unresolved, and we have not established grounds for determining or even understanding degrees of embodiment within this conception.

**Discussion provenance:** SE proposed changes in embodied experience and activity across a lifetime, including walking with a stick and changing experiences of running, as meaningful for thinking about degrees. LLM distinguished describable changes from inferences or assumptions about degrees. We did not establish how degrees could be understood or determined.

### 2. Lived body / phenomenology

**Conception:** Lived body / phenomenology. Embodiment understood through the body as lived and experienced, including sensation, perception and movement (provisional characterisation of our map category).

**Account considered:** Sondra Horton Fraleigh (1987), *Dance and the Lived Body: A Descriptive Aesthetics*. Selected because it brings phenomenological thinking into the experience of dancing. Fraleigh has **not** been verified as endorsing the equivalence between bodily sensitivity and embodiment.

**Agreed provisional finding (SE–LLM, not Fraleigh's claim):**

> Within the conception of lived body / phenomenology, degrees of embodiment may be intelligible through changes in bodily sensitivity developed through sustained somatic and embodied practices. From an experiential standpoint, increased sensitivity can meaningfully be described as increased embodiment. However, the equivalence between sensitivity and embodiment remains situated and is not established across phenomenological accounts. The relationship between deliberate attention, cultivated sensitivity and habitual bodily awareness warrants further examination but does not prevent a provisional finding.

**Discussion provenance:** SE described forty years of dancing and greater bodily sensitivity developed through sustained attention in multiple somatic and embodied practices, proposing sensitivity as a shorthand for embodiment. SE also distinguished change in perception from perception of change and suggested that the connection extends beyond dance, for example to meditation. LLM proposed that cultivated sensitivity might become available without deliberate attention. This possibility was not resolved. Some relevant scholarship on bodily/interoceptive awareness was identified for future verification and reading, not used as proof that phenomenology generally equates sensitivity and embodiment.
