# Embodiment as connection to the senses

## Quotation

> We evolved from animals, and we require a connection to our senses in order to feel safe, comfortable, happy, and joyous. This is what people are talking about when they talk about “embodiment.” Embodiment means being in touch with the lush world of the senses. The colors of light. The nuances of sound. The textures of touch. This is where connection to the world, the present moment, and the body exist. People use booze or other sedatives to knock their endless conceptual blather offline and get into their body, but this is a brute-force method with a lot of drawbacks, like hangovers and addiction. Plus it partially defeats itself, since it tends to be numbing to be the body, too. Drink may get you back in your senses, but your senses will be foggy and fuzzy.

## Context

**Source-based paraphrase:** In 'Sensory Clarity', Taft contrasts attending to immediate sensory experience with becoming absorbed in conceptual thinking. In the adjacent text he argues that mindfulness meditation can help a practitioner reconnect with the senses without relying on intoxicants. His statement about what people mean by 'embodiment' is his own usage, not demonstrated here as a general consensus.

## Relevance

**Potential boundary-test relevance:** BT-04 – Degrees of embodiment.

**LLM interpretation (provisional):** This is a comparatively direct example of an author defining embodiment in terms of sensory contact. It may be relevant to SE's proposal that increased bodily sensitivity can, within certain practice traditions, be understood as increased embodiment. Taft's account includes senses directed towards the environment, not solely sensations of the body. The quotation does not itself specify how degrees are established.

**Possible map connections (not findings):** Lived body / phenomenology; interoception / feeling / selfhood. The latter would require care because Taft's examples include exteroceptive sensing. No conception has been assigned definitively.
