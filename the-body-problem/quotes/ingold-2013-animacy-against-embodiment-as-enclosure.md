# Animacy against embodiment as enclosure

## Quotation

> Comparison of the two pieces vividly demonstrates how animacy and embodiment pull in opposite directions: where the former is a movement of opening, the latter is bent on closure. For the living, animate beings we are, argues dance philosopher Maxine Sheets-Johnstone, the term ‘embodiment’ is simply not experientially apposite. We do not, she insists, experience ourselves and one another as ‘packaged’ but as moving and moved, in ongoing response – that is in correspondence – with the things around us (Sheets-Johnstone 1998: 359; Ingold 2011b: 10).

## Context

**Source-based paraphrase:** Ingold contrasts two works discussed immediately before this passage, describing one as closed into the shape of a body and the other as open to exchanges with its surrounding medium. He then argues that ‘animacy’ is a better descriptor of living beings' ongoing movement and correspondence with their surroundings than an account of embodiment understood as being contained in a body. Ingold invokes Sheets-Johnstone's criticism of ‘embodiment’ in support of the argument. The formulation ‘animacy and embodiment pull in opposite directions’ is Ingold's presentation; the passage attributes a related experiential objection to Sheets-Johnstone. Her own 1998 text would need consulting before treating Ingold's précis as her full position.

## Relevance

**Project-wide boundary question:** This challenges whether ‘embodiment’ is always an appropriate organising term at all, rather than proposing one more definition to place on the map. It bears on the project's allowance for genuinely outside accounts and its caution against expanding categories to accommodate every case.

**Possible map connection (not assigned):** Relationality / sense-making, insofar as ongoing responsiveness and correspondence are at issue. But fitting Ingold's language into that category risks erasing his explicit resistance to ‘embodiment’ as a term.

**LLM interpretation (provisional):** The passage may provide a useful test of the boundary between an account omitted from the map and one that rejects the framing of the map itself.
