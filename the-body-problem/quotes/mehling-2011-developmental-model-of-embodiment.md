# A developmental model of embodiment beyond the biomedical mind–body split

## Quotation

> In addition, these findings suggest that we need to broaden the biomedical paradigm to consider and include a developmental model of embodiment in order to better understand how body awareness-enhancing therapies work [52,63]. This paradigmatic model has been proposed for several decades in philosophical and nursing literature [52,63]. It overcomes the mind-body split, as it still persists in the biomedical model and integrates the phenomenology of complex mind-body interactions, as they are experienced by practitioners and patients in body awareness-enhancing therapies.

## Context

**Source-based paraphrase:** Mehling and colleagues report qualitative focus-group research with practitioners and patients of various body awareness-enhancing therapies. They propose that biomedical explanations should attend to a developmental model of embodiment that acknowledges practitioners' and patients' experiences, rather than retaining a mind–body split. The developmental model is presented as an implication of their research, supported by earlier philosophical and nursing literature, not as a theory newly established by this study.

## Relevance

**Boundary test:** BT-04 – Degrees of embodiment (potential relevance).

**LLM interpretation (provisional):** A developmental model offers a way to investigate change in embodied self-awareness across practice. The notion of development should not be silently taken as proof of more or less embodiment, or as a single quantitative scale. This is also relevant to the methodological relationship between phenomenological and practice-oriented approaches raised in BT-04.
