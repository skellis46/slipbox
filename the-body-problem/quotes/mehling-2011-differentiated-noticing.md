# Mehling – differentiated noticing as a path to integration

## Quotation

> A central skill that patients learn through training and repetition is the ability to notice sensations, thoughts and feelings as they occur in their actual immediacy. What is noticed might be verbalized or not. The point is that the process of noticing and the learning of differentiated noticing were viewed by the practitioners as a path to integration.

## Context

**Source-based paraphrase:** The authors report practitioners' view that learning to notice, and to notice in a differentiated way, is a path to integration. Noticing may or may not be put into words.

## Relevance

**LLM (Claude) interpretations (provisional, not agreed):**
- **Attribution matters for C09.** Here the claim is reported as the *practitioners'* view and the outcome is *integration*. In the p. 7 passage the authors summarise it as leading to 'embodiment and integration'. So 'embodiment' as an achievement may be the authors' gloss, not the practitioners' term. Check this before treating Mehling as a source for embodiment-as-achievement.
- **'Might be verbalized or not':** noticing is treated as a skill independent of verbal articulation. Relevant to C06 (bodily knowing and articulation), BT-06 and C07b.
- **BT-06:** training in differentiated noticing resembles Ingold's education of attention.
- **C08:** cultivated noticing as a skill, though in a therapeutic, outcome-oriented setting.

**Possible map connections (not findings):** interoception / feeling / selfhood; lived body / phenomenology.
